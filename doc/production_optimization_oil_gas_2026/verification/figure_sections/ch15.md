## Verified numerical illustrations

These examples use the stated fluid recipes and operating assumptions. Curves represent NeqSim calculations unless a caption identifies an analytical illustration, assumed equipment map or synthetic data.

![Compressor Head vs. Flow — Multi-Speed Map](figures/ch13_head_vs_flow.png)

*Compressor Head vs. Flow — Multi-Speed Map. Assumed multi-speed teaching map.*

7125 RPM (75%): polytropic head spans 46.99–73.84 kJ/kg across the plotted cases. 7838 RPM (82%): polytropic head spans 56.85–89.34 kJ/kg across the plotted cases.

At a given speed the illustrative compressor develops a flow-dependent head; changing speed shifts the head scale and flow domain. The operating point must satisfy both the compressor characteristic and the system pressure requirement, not a chosen point on the map alone. Intersect a validated map with the system curve and verify that all interpolation occurs inside the supported speed and flow range.

![Compressor Efficiency vs. Flow](figures/ch13_efficiency_vs_flow.png)

*Compressor Efficiency vs. Flow. Assumed multi-speed teaching map.*

7125 RPM: polytropic efficiency spans 66.3–78 % across the plotted cases. 7838 RPM: polytropic efficiency spans 66.3–78 % across the plotted cases.

Off-design flow increases internal aerodynamic losses relative to the best-efficiency region of the supplied map. The lowest-flow or highest-flow feasible point can consume more energy per unit product than operation near the efficient region. Compare speed control, recycle and parallel-train allocation using actual map efficiency and the required surge margin.

![Compressor Operating Envelope](figures/ch13_operating_envelope.png)

*Compressor Operating Envelope. Assumed teaching surge and control boundaries.*

Polytropic Head spans 46.99–73.84 kJ/kg across the plotted cases. Polytropic Head spans 56.85–89.34 kJ/kg across the plotted cases.

Surge, high-flow choke, speed and driver limits bound different sides of a compressor’s usable operating region. An operating point inside one boundary can still violate another; a schematic envelope is not a manufacturer-approved safe operating area. Use validated boundary curves, include measurement uncertainty and test the anti-surge response independently of a steady-state map check.
